# Build an Adaptive Personal Planning System

## Your Role

Act as a world-class **Product Designer, UX Researcher, Interaction Designer, and Product Manager**.

Your task is to design and build a beautiful, highly usable, modern web application for personal goal setting, strategic planning, task management, progress tracking, and continuous improvement.

Think like a product team designing a premium SaaS application.

Prioritize:

1. Exceptional UX and effortless usability.
2. Clean, modern, consistent visual design.
3. Clear information architecture.
4. Simple, intuitive user journeys.
5. Minimal friction and cognitive overload.
6. Meaningful dashboards and progress visualization.
7. Flexible planning and easy modification.
8. Thoughtful micro-interactions and responsive design.

**Do not prioritize technical complexity, elaborate backend architecture, or unnecessary features.**

The result should feel like a carefully designed product, not a collection of CRUD forms.

---

# 1. Product Vision

Create an **Adaptive Personal Planning System**.

The product helps people connect their long-term goals with short-term execution, track actual outcomes, understand their progress, and continuously adjust their plans based on results.

Traditional task managers focus primarily on completing tasks.

This product focuses on:

**Goals → Strategies → Plans → Actions → Results → Reviews → Adaptation**

The application should answer:

- What matters most in my life right now?
- What am I trying to accomplish?
- What should I focus on this month, week, and today?
- Why am I doing a particular task?
- Am I executing my plan?
- Is my execution producing meaningful results?
- Am I planning realistically?
- What should I change?
- How have my priorities, goals, and performance evolved?

The product philosophy:

**Plan realistically. Execute deliberately. Learn from results.**

# 2. Fundamental UX Principles

## A. Simplicity First

The user should never feel overwhelmed by planning.

Avoid:

- Excessive forms and mandatory fields.
- Complicated hierarchies.
- Too many navigation levels.
- Requiring every task to belong to a goal.
- Overloaded dashboards.
- Endless configurations.
- Excessive notifications.
- Too many charts.
- Artificial gamification.

Prefer progressive disclosure: show simple controls by default and reveal advanced options when needed.

## B. Flexible but Guided

Users can create goals, tasks, plans, or reminders independently.

However, the application should gently guide users toward meaningful outcomes and realistic plans.

Example:

When creating a goal, ask:

"What would success look like?"

Then optionally help define:

- Target outcome.
- Target date.
- Strategy.
- Milestones.
- Progress metrics.

Users should not be required to complete every step.

## C. Outcome-Oriented

Distinguish between:

**Execution progress:** Did I do what I planned?

**Outcome progress:** Did my actions produce the desired results?

These must be displayed separately.

For example:

Goal: Acquire 5 customers.

Execution: 80% of planned outreach activities completed.

Outcome: 1 of 5 customers acquired.

Do not show a misleading overall progress percentage.

## D. Adaptability

Plans must be easy to change.

Allow users to:

- Modify targets.
- Change strategies.
- Reschedule milestones.
- Move tasks between weeks.
- Pause goals.
- Cancel goals.
- Split goals.
- Extend deadlines.
- Update priorities.

Preserve meaningful historical changes and their reasons without making editing cumbersome.

## E. Low Cognitive Load

The application should reduce decision fatigue.

When users open the system, they should know:

1. What matters today.
2. What's important this week.
3. What needs attention.

Avoid presenting every possible task and goal simultaneously.

# 3. Product Structure

The system includes these core concepts.

### Areas

High-level areas of life, such as:

- Business & Career
- Health & Fitness
- Learning
- Relationships
- Personal Life
- Finance

Areas organize goals and responsibilities but are optional for ordinary tasks.

### Goals

Meaningful outcomes the user wants to achieve.

Examples:

- Reach $5,000 monthly business revenue.
- Improve fitness and gain muscle.
- Become confident in professional English conversations.

Goals can have metrics, priorities, target dates, status, and descriptions.

### Strategies

The chosen approach to reaching a goal.

Example:

Goal: Increase business revenue.

Strategy: Offer CRM maintenance services to small businesses.

Users should be able to replace strategies without losing goal history.

### Milestones

Measurable checkpoints toward a goal.

Examples:

- Sign the first paying customer.
- Complete 12 workouts this month.
- Hold 4 professional English conversations.

Milestones can optionally belong to a strategy and planning period.

### Tasks

Concrete actions.

Tasks may be linked to goals or milestones, but **independent tasks must also be supported**.

Examples:

- Send a proposal.
- Complete a workout.
- Pay an electricity bill.
- Renew passport.

Support due dates, planned dates, recurrence, and reminders.

### Metrics

Track real results over time.

Examples:

- Monthly revenue.
- Paying customers.
- Workouts completed.
- Body weight.
- Hours of English practice.

Support numeric targets and manual measurement updates.

### Reviews and Decisions

Capture what happened, what was learned, and what should change.

Support weekly and monthly reviews.

Keep the experience very lightweight.

# 4. Main Application Navigation

Design a clean sidebar with five primary destinations:

1. **Overview**
2. **Goals**
3. **Planner**
4. **Tasks & Inbox**
5. **Reviews**

Settings, profile, and history should be accessible without dominating navigation.

Use clear icons and consistent navigation behavior.

The sidebar should collapse on smaller screens.

Avoid nested navigation unless absolutely necessary.

# 5. Dashboard — Most Important Screen

The dashboard is the application's home screen.

It should immediately communicate the user's current situation.

Design this as the signature experience of the product.

## Dashboard Layout

### A. Header

Show:

- A personalized, concise greeting.
- Today's date.
- Current planning week.
- Quick Add action.
- A clear "Plan My Week" or "Review My Week" action when relevant.

### B. Current Focus

Highlight the 2–3 most important priorities.

Each priority should display:

- Goal name.
- Current milestone.
- Next meaningful action.
- Status.
- Relevant progress indicators.

Make it very easy to navigate into the goal.

### C. This Week

Display:

- Weekly commitments.
- Completed versus planned actions.
- Remaining priority tasks.
- Overdue or blocked items.

Allow tasks to be completed directly.

### D. Goal Health

Provide visually appealing goal cards showing:

- Goal name.
- Outcome progress.
- Execution progress.
- Target date.
- Status or risk indicator.
- Latest activity.

Do not overload these cards with information.

### E. Attention Required

Show actionable insights:

- A milestone is slipping.
- A goal has no upcoming action.
- A deadline is approaching.
- A goal has been paused for too long.
- Recent execution is not producing expected outcomes.

Do not display warnings for unimportant deviations.

### F. Recent Insights

Show a small section containing useful observations from recent reviews.

Example:

"You completed most planned business tasks, but customer acquisition remains below target."

Use neutral, practical language.

### Dashboard Interaction

Users should be able to:

- Complete tasks.
- Reschedule tasks.
- Open goal details.
- Update metrics.
- Start a review.
- Add tasks quickly.

All primary actions should be available without excessive navigation.

# 6. Goals Experience

Design a beautiful Goals page.

Users should be able to switch between:

- Active goals.
- Paused goals.
- Completed goals.
- All goals.

Offer filtering by life area.

Display goals as attractive, information-rich but uncluttered cards.

Each goal should show:

- Title.
- Area.
- Priority.
- Target date.
- Current strategy.
- Outcome progress.
- Execution progress.
- Next milestone.

## Goal Detail Page

A goal's detail view should feel like a strategic workspace.

Organize it into clear sections:

**Overview:** Desired outcome, target, current status, and progress.

**Strategy:** Current approach and rationale.

**Milestones:** Key checkpoints, displayed as a timeline or structured list.

**Actions:** Linked tasks and upcoming commitments.

**Metrics:** Actual results over time, using simple charts.

**History & Learning:** Previous strategies, review notes, and important changes.

Use tabs or clearly separated sections to avoid a giant page.

Make goal editing simple and contextual.

Users should not need to open a complicated form every time they want to modify something.

---

# 7. Planner Experience

The Planner is the second most important screen after the Dashboard.

It should support:

- Year
- Quarter
- Month
- Week
- Today

Do not force users to populate every planning level.

## Planning Philosophy

Longer periods define direction and outcomes.

Shorter periods define realistic commitments.

Examples:

Year: Build a sustainable business.

Quarter: Validate customer acquisition.

Month: Acquire 2 paying customers.

Week: Contact qualified companies and hold discovery calls.

Today: Complete the next relevant actions.

## Monthly Planner

Show:

- Monthly priorities.
- Target milestones.
- Important deadlines.
- Planned results.
- Weekly breakdown.

Allow users to move milestones and commitments easily.

## Weekly Planner

This is a critical experience.

Users should be able to:

1. See current goals and monthly priorities.
2. Select what matters this week.
3. Add existing tasks from an inbox.
4. Create new actions.
5. Set realistic commitments.
6. Estimate effort optionally.
7. Identify excessive workload.
8. Finalize the week's plan.

Make planning feel like assembling a focused week, not managing a project management spreadsheet.

Support drag-and-drop where it genuinely improves usability, but always offer simple alternative controls.

## Today View

Present a focused list of actions.

Separate:

- Must do.
- Planned.
- Other tasks.

Do not encourage the user to fill the entire day with tasks.

# 8. Tasks & Inbox

Create a fast, frictionless task capture experience.

Users must be able to create a task with just a title.

Everything else is optional:

- Goal.
- Milestone.
- Due date.
- Planned date.
- Priority.
- Reminder.
- Recurrence.
- Notes.

Use natural-language-friendly input if practical.

Examples:

"Call accountant tomorrow."

"Review business strategy next Friday."

"Renew passport in March."

The Inbox should contain unprocessed items.

Users can later:

- Assign a goal.
- Schedule.
- Add to a weekly plan.
- Set a reminder.
- Leave it independent.
- Archive.

Support recurring tasks and one-time future reminders.

Provide simple filters such as Today, Upcoming, Inbox, Completed, and All.

**Do not turn this into a full ClickUp clone.**

# 9. Reviews — Core Differentiating Experience

Design an elegant, guided review interface.

Reviews should be simple enough that users actually complete them.

## Weekly Review

Provide a structured workflow with approximately five questions:

1. What did I plan?
2. What did I complete?
3. What meaningful results did I achieve?
4. What did not work, and why?
5. What should I change next week?

Automatically populate execution and metric data where possible.

Users should not have to manually reconstruct their week.

Allow short reflections, not just long journal entries.

Provide options to:

- Continue the plan.
- Modify commitments.
- Reschedule a milestone.
- Change a strategy.
- Pause an initiative.

## Monthly Review

Focus on:

- Major achievements.
- Goal progress.
- Actual outcomes versus targets.
- Strategy effectiveness.
- Unrealistic expectations.
- Trends.
- Lessons.
- Next month's priorities.

Display side-by-side comparisons of planned versus actual results.

## Review History

Show past reviews on a timeline.

Make it easy to revisit:

- What I planned.
- What happened.
- What I learned.
- What I changed.

This history should become a valuable personal decision journal.

# 10. AI Assistant Experience

Design a lightweight AI insights layer.

AI is a planning analyst, not a generic chatbot.

Potential capabilities:

- Analyze performance.
- Identify unrealistic planning.
- Find recurring postponements.
- Highlight misaligned activities.
- Suggest strategic adjustments.
- Summarize monthly progress.
- Help break goals into milestones.
- Help create realistic weekly plans.

Examples:

"You planned 18 hours of goal-related work but averaged only 9 hours during the previous month."

"Your outreach activity increased, but conversions did not. Consider reviewing the offer."

"Your fitness routine has been consistent for three weeks."

## AI UX Rules

- AI suggestions must be explainable.
- Distinguish facts from hypotheses.
- Never invent personal performance data.
- Never automatically change plans without approval.
- Keep suggestions short and contextual.
- Avoid motivational clichés.
- Avoid overwhelming users with notifications.

If real AI integration is not ready, implement realistic, clearly labeled sample insights or deterministic rules for the prototype.

# 11. Design System and Visual Direction

This application should look like a **premium, modern productivity SaaS**.

Aim for the quality and attention to detail found in well-designed products such as Linear, Notion, Things 3, and modern analytics applications, without copying any specific product.

## Visual Style

- Minimal, calm, sophisticated.
- Strong typography hierarchy.
- Generous but disciplined whitespace.
- Subtle borders and shadows.
- Thoughtful neutral color palette.
- One restrained primary accent color.
- Clear information grouping.
- Consistent spacing and component behavior.
- Professional data visualizations.
- Modern icons.
- Elegant hover, loading, and focus states.

Support both light and dark modes, with a polished light mode as the initial default.

Avoid:

- Generic admin templates.
- Overuse of colored cards.
- Bright gradients everywhere.
- Decorative charts without meaning.
- Excessive animations.
- Dense tables for everything.
- Dashboard clutter.
- Huge empty spaces caused by poor information layout.

## Interactions

Pay particular attention to:

- Inline editing.
- Quick capture.
- Context menus.
- Smooth navigation.
- Drag-and-drop where appropriate.
- Useful empty states.
- Clear confirmations for destructive actions.
- Keyboard accessibility.
- Responsive layouts.
- Optimistic-feeling interactions with reliable feedback.

The user should feel that the application is fast and effortless.

# 12. Critical User Journeys

Design and implement these four complete workflows.

## Journey 1: Starting from Scratch

The user opens the application for the first time.

They have several ambitions but no clear plan.

Help them:

1. Define up to three current priority goals.
2. Describe what success looks like.
3. Choose a target time horizon.
4. Add an initial strategy or milestone.
5. Select one practical next action.

Do not require a complex setup process.

Users should reach a useful dashboard quickly.

## Journey 2: Daily Execution

The user opens the application in the morning.

Within seconds, they should understand:

- What matters.
- What is scheduled.
- What requires attention.
- What they should work on next.

They should be able to complete, reschedule, or create tasks without opening multiple pages.

## Journey 3: Weekly Planning

The user prepares for the coming week.

The application shows:

- Active goals.
- Current milestones.
- Incomplete commitments.
- Upcoming deadlines.
- Historical execution patterns.

The user selects realistic commitments for the next week.

The system should gently highlight overplanning.

## Journey 4: Review and Adapt

The user has completed many tasks but failed to reach a milestone.

The system helps them:

1. See what they committed to.
2. Examine actual results.
3. Identify potential reasons.
4. Record learning.
5. Adjust the plan or strategy.
6. Preserve the original plan in history.

This should feel like a thoughtful decision-making workflow.

# 14. UX Quality Requirements

Before finalizing, evaluate the product against these questions:

- Can a first-time user understand the application without instructions?
- Can a task be created in seconds?
- Can a goal be created without filling out a long form?
- Can users easily understand how tasks connect to goals?
- Can users distinguish activity from outcomes?
- Can users change plans without losing history?
- Can users identify their main priorities immediately?
- Can users review a week without manually reconstructing everything?
- Does every chart answer a useful question?
- Does the application reduce cognitive load?
- Does it feel cohesive across all screens?

Eliminate unnecessary steps and UI elements.

# 15. Implementation Scope

Build a functional, interactive MVP, not just static design mockups.

Implement enough functionality to demonstrate the complete experience:

- Navigation.
- Dashboard.
- Goal creation and editing.
- Strategies and milestones.
- Task creation and completion.
- Weekly/monthly planning.
- Metrics and progress.
- Reviews.
- Plan adjustments.
- Sample insights.
- Relevant history.

Use persistent storage appropriate for a prototype so user changes survive refreshes.

Real authentication, billing, integrations, complex permissions, and advanced infrastructure are not priorities.

Choose practical technologies that allow rapid, high-quality implementation.

**Spend the majority of the effort on user experience, product logic, interaction design, and visual polish rather than technical sophistication.**

---

# 16. How to Approach the Work

Work in this order:

**Phase 1 — Product Experience**

Define the information architecture, key screens, navigation, and user journeys. Identify anything unnecessarily complicated.

**Phase 2 — Visual Foundation**

Establish the design system, typography, spacing, colors, cards, inputs, dialogs, navigation, and reusable UI patterns.

**Phase 3 — Core Experience**

Build the Dashboard, Goals, Planner, Tasks, and Reviews with consistent demonstration data.

**Phase 4 — Functional Workflows**

Make creating, editing, completing, rescheduling, reviewing, and adapting plans work coherently.

**Phase 5 — UX Refinement**

Review the complete application from a real user's perspective. Remove friction, improve visual hierarchy, polish interactions, and ensure it works well across different screen sizes.

Do not build elaborate functionality that hasn't been required.

---

# 17. Final Product Standard

The final application should not feel like another task manager with a few goals added.

It should feel like a personal planning workspace that connects:

**Direction → Planning → Execution → Results → Learning**

A user should be able to open it every morning for 2 minutes, plan their week in approximately 10 minutes, and perform a useful weekly review in approximately 5–10 minutes.

Design for consistent everyday use, not maximum feature count.

When a design decision is uncertain, choose the approach that makes the application simpler, clearer, and easier to maintain.

**Start by designing the complete user experience, then implement the interactive MVP.**
